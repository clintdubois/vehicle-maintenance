# 2014 Honda CR-V — VTC Actuator Replacement (Cold Start Rattle)

Source: **Honda Service Bulletin 16-012, Version 2** (Feb 23, 2017, supersedes the Feb 13, 2016 original) — the current, correct bulletin for this vehicle. It explicitly lists "2014 CR-V 2WD/4WD — ALL" in its affected-vehicles table, so this is the right procedure, not the older 2014-dated bulletin 09-010 (which only covers through the 2012 CR-V and uses an outdated part number).

## Symptom

At cold start-up, the engine rattles loudly for about 2 seconds. May be intermittent, and tends to occur when the outside temperature is below 40°F.

## Cause

The variable valve timing control (VTC) actuator is defective — on cold start, before oil pressure builds (2-3 seconds), the actuator's internal gear has enough play to rattle against the camshaft.

## Confirm the diagnosis before ordering parts

1. Don't start the engine for **at least 6 hours** (lets oil fully drain from the VTC system).
2. Start the engine and listen immediately.
3. A loud rattle lasting ~2 seconds on startup confirms the diagnosis — proceed with the repair below.
4. If you hear a different noise, or nothing abnormal, this isn't the fix — don't proceed on assumption alone.

**Worth knowing**: some sources note VTC rattle can also be a symptom of timing chain stretch, not just a bad actuator. Before buying parts, it's worth inspecting the timing chain tensioner (small inspection cover on the timing case) — if the chain itself has stretched, replacing just the actuator may not fully resolve the noise.

Plus standard shop tools: torque wrench (must read up to 113 N·m/83 lb-ft), open-end wrench for holding the camshaft, jack/lift access, wire ties, electrical tape, compressed air source.

**Do not use an impact wrench on the actuator mounting bolt** — Honda's bulletin explicitly warns this causes internal damage to the actuator housing, vanes, or lock pin. Torque wrench only.

## Repair procedure

Official flat-rate time is 2.0 hours for a trained tech — budget significantly more as a DIY job, especially your first time through. This is a real engine-timing job (cylinder head cover off, camshaft removed), not a bolt-on part swap.

### Prep / access

1. Remove the frame (strut) brace, if equipped.
2. Remove the engine cover.
3. Remove the ignition coil cover and the 4 ignition coils (bolts: 12 N·m/9 lb-ft).
4. Remove the engine oil dipstick; disconnect the breather hose and brake booster vacuum hose from the cylinder head cover.
5. Remove the two bolts holding the EVAP canister purge valve bracket to the cylinder head.
6. Remove the cylinder head cover.
7. Raise the vehicle on a lift.
8. Turn the front wheels, or remove the right front wheel.
9. Remove the splash shield for access to the crankshaft pulley.
10. Remove the camshaft auto-tensioner (chain case) cover.

### Lock the auto-tensioner and mark the timing chain

11. Rotate the crankshaft **clockwise** to top dead center (TDC), No. 1 cylinder.
12. Rotate the crankshaft **counterclockwise** to compress the auto-tensioner. Align the hole in the lock and auto-tensioner, then insert the 1.2mm stopper (lock pin).
13. Rotate the crankshaft **clockwise** back to TDC No. 1.
14. Lower the vehicle.
15. **Mark the timing chain** at the punch marks on the exhaust camshaft sprocket and VTC actuator. Secure the chain to the sprocket with a wire tie — this reference mark is what lets you reinstall correctly timed later.

### Remove the camshaft/actuator assembly

16. Loosen the camshaft holder bolts, in sequence, **two turns at a time** (don't fully loosen one before moving to the next — even, gradual release).
17. Remove the five camshaft holders. Set them aside on a clean surface, **in the exact order and position** they came off — they're not universally interchangeable.
18. Carefully tip up the end of the intake camshaft until there's enough slack for an assistant to lift the chain off the VTC actuator teeth.
19. Remove the intake camshaft/VTC actuator assembly while your assistant keeps light tension on the chain. Set it on a clean, padded workbench.
20. Secure the timing chain to the A/C compressor hose with a wire/zip tie so it doesn't fall into the front cover.

### Swap the actuator (on the bench)

21. With an assistant holding the camshaft steady with an open-end wrench, remove the VTC actuator mounting bolt and separate the old actuator from the camshaft. **Do not reuse it.**
22. Confirm the new actuator is in the **unlocked** position before installing.
23. Install the new, unlocked actuator onto the camshaft — don't force it on.
24. With the actuator facing you, rotate it **counterclockwise** until it engages the camshaft's locator pin. Installed this way, it should not lock. Apply clean engine oil to the mounting bolt threads, install the bolt finger-tight.
   - **If it's unlocked after this** → skip to step 28.
   - **If it locked anyway** → continue to step 25 to unlock it.
25. To unlock: apply clean engine oil to the mounting bolt threads, install the bolt finger-tight on the actuator.
26. Cover **pressure port #1** on the camshaft with several wraps of electrical tape, secured with a zip tie. **Leave pressure port #2 uncovered.**
27. Apply compressed air to pressure port #2. While air is applied, rotate the actuator by hand to the unlocked position.
28. With an assistant holding the assembly steady, **torque the mounting bolt to 113 N·m (83 lb-ft)** with a torque wrench. Be careful not to scratch/damage the camshaft — and again, **no impact wrench**.
29. After torquing, rotate the actuator to the **locked** position.
30. Remove the wire ties while your assistant keeps light tension on the chain.

### Reinstall

31. Slide the camshaft/actuator assembly in at an angle so the chain can slip over the actuator's teeth.
32. Line up the marks you made in step 15 with the punch marks on the exhaust camshaft sprocket and VTC actuator.
33. Apply engine oil to the journals and caps for both camshafts. Lower the assembly onto its journals.
34. Install the camshaft holders, tightening **two turns at a time**, starting in the middle and working outward, following the numbered sequence (see torque table below — CR-V and Crosstour have slightly different bolt patterns, use the CR-V diagram from the bulletin).

   **Torque**: 8×1.25mm bolts → 22 N·m (16 lb-ft); 6×1.0mm bolts → 12 N·m (9 lb-ft)

35. Remove the lock pin from the auto-tensioner.
36. Rotate the crankshaft clockwise **two full turns**, then stop at the TDC mark — use the correct timing marks (the bulletin explicitly shows which mark to use vs. a nearby decoy mark **not** to use). Confirm both camshafts line up as they did in step 15.
37. Inside the cylinder block, visually confirm the timing chain is riding properly on its guide and hasn't slipped behind it. Realign if needed.
38. Clean off old sealant, dry, reseal, and reinstall the auto-tensioner (chain case) cover. **Wait 30 minutes before adding oil (if needed), then wait 3 hours before starting the engine** — the sealant needs to cure.

### Valve clearance (while you're in there)

39. Check valve clearance with the tappet adjuster; adjust if needed.
   - Intake: 0.21–0.25mm (0.008–0.010 in)
   - Exhaust: 0.25–0.29mm (0.010–0.011 in)
40. Torque the locknuts (intake and exhaust) to 14 N·m (10 lb-ft) using the tappet locknut wrench.

### Buttoning up

41. Raise the vehicle.
42. Reinstall the splash shield.
43. Straighten the wheels or reinstall the right front wheel.
44. Lower the vehicle.
45. Check spark plug seals for damage; replace if needed. Check the head cover gasket; replace if needed.
46. Install the head cover gasket in its groove.
47. Remove all old liquid gasket from the chain case and the No. 5 rocker shaft holder.
48. Clean the head cover contacting surfaces with a clean shop towel.
49. Reinstall the cylinder head cover, tightening bolts in three steps — final step torques all bolts, in sequence, to 12 N·m (8.7 lb-ft).
50. Reinstall the EVAP canister purge valve bracket.
51. Reinstall the dipstick, breather hose, and brake booster vacuum hose.
52. Reinstall the ignition coils and ignition coil cover (12 N·m/9 lb-ft).
53. Reinstall the engine cover.
54. Reinstall the frame (strut) brace, if equipped — torque to 22 N·m (16 lb-ft).

## Key warnings, gathered from the bulletin

- **Do not reuse the old VTC actuator** once removed.
- **Do not use an impact wrench** on the actuator mounting bolt — causes internal damage to the housing, vanes, or lock pin. Torque wrench only, to 113 N·m (83 lb-ft).
- **Camshaft holders go back in the exact order/position they came out** — don't mix them up.
- **Use the correct timing mark** at the crankshaft pulley in step 36 — the bulletin specifically illustrates a nearby mark that looks similar but is wrong.
- **Wait 3 hours after resealing the chain case cover before starting the engine** — don't rush this to "just check if it worked."
- This is fundamentally a **timing job** — if the camshaft timing marks aren't realigned exactly right in steps 15/32/36, you risk a running engine that's out of time, which can cause real damage. Not a step to eyeball if you're not confident.

## Before you start — verify against your actual VIN

The bulletin's affected-vehicles table lists specific VIN ranges for 2013 model-year CR-Vs (varies by plant code), but states "2014 CR-V 2WD/4WD — ALL," so your 2014 should be covered outright. Worth a quick double check against your actual VIN if you want to be certain, since Honda bulletins are VIN-range-specific by design.
